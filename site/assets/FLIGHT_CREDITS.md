# Flight visualization

- `earth_2048.jpg`: `earth_atmos_2048.jpg` from the Three.js examples texture collection, https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/textures/planets/earth_atmos_2048.jpg (retrieved 2026-11-14). The Three.js repository uses the MIT license: https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/LICENSE .
- `moon_color_4k.webp`: existing 4096×2048 lunar color texture derived from NASA Scientific Visualization Studio's LROC color poles image, https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720. The project conversion is documented in `site/tools/make_textures.py`; this animation reuses the local asset without downloading or duplicating it. The equirectangular map is sampled in sRGB with horizontal wrapping, clamped poles, trilinear mipmaps and the renderer's maximum supported anisotropic filtering.
- Lander models and their existing provenance: `models/README.md`.
- Camera reference: NASA Scientific Visualization Studio, “LRO Spacecraft Animations,” https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/20140/ and “Lunar Fly By and Earth Approach,” https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/3041/ . No video assets are embedded or redistributed.

This 28-second flight uses one continuous scene. The opening camera looks back at Earth as the lander leaves, then swings toward the Moon and follows the existing braking turn into vertical descent. The lander is deliberately enlarged to a regional scale, so its final silhouette remains readable while the camera shows the surrounding craters. On touchdown it stays on screen until Exit is chosen; nothing advances automatically.

The landing surface is the measured LOLA/SLDEM2015 terrain already shipped with the Atlas. The flight uses the 80/160 m site patches or south-polar LOLA levels where available and the whole-Moon elevation map beyond them. There is no generated close-up disc replacing the terrain. Additional mesh sampling does not increase the source data's physical resolution. The camera, enlarged lander and Earth placement are arranged for the demonstration: the final camera faces Earth with the lander in the foreground. Positions, sizes, propulsion, orientation, illumination, Earth placement and travel time are illustrative and do not represent a simulated mission trajectory or the selected date's Earth visibility. The scientific views and their calculations are unchanged.
