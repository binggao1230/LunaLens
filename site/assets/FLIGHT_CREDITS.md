# Flight visualization

- `earth_day_4k.webp`: the existing 4096×2048 NASA GIBS Blue Marble shaded-relief texture used by the Mission globe. Source request and conversion are recorded in `site/tools/make_textures.py` (`BlueMarble_ShadedRelief_Bathymetry`); the flight reuses this local file.
- `moon_color_4k.webp`: existing 4096×2048 lunar color texture derived from NASA Scientific Visualization Studio's LROC color poles image, https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720. The project conversion is documented in `site/tools/make_textures.py`; this animation reuses the local asset without downloading or duplicating it. The equirectangular map is sampled in sRGB with horizontal wrapping, clamped poles, trilinear mipmaps and the renderer's maximum supported anisotropic filtering.
- Lander models and their existing provenance: `models/README.md`.
- Camera reference: NASA Scientific Visualization Studio, “LRO Spacecraft Animations,” https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/20140/ and “Lunar Fly By and Earth Approach,” https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/3041/ . No video assets are embedded or redistributed.

This 28-second flight uses one continuous scene. Earth sits to the left of the departing lander and the complete Moon is visible farther away to the right. The established departure camera, pacing and braking turn into vertical descent are retained. Earth is arranged for the lunar shot while outside the camera view, then its position and radius hold steady as the original tracking camera reveals it during approach. The lander is deliberately enlarged to a regional scale, so its final silhouette remains readable from the wider crater panorama. On touchdown it stays on screen until Exit is chosen; nothing advances automatically.

The landing surface is the measured LOLA/SLDEM2015 terrain already shipped with the Atlas. The flight uses the 80/160 m site patches or south-polar LOLA levels where available. The distant polar skyline also uses the existing regional LOLA L0 raster (296.875 m source pixels), falling back to the whole-Moon elevation map outside its coverage. Rendering samples and mesh spacing are coarser than the finest source pixels. There is no generated close-up disc replacing the terrain. Additional mesh sampling does not increase the source data's physical resolution. The camera, enlarged lander and Earth placement are arranged for the demonstration: the final camera faces Earth with the lander in the foreground. Positions, sizes, propulsion, orientation, illumination, Earth placement and travel time are illustrative and do not represent a simulated mission trajectory or the selected date's Earth visibility. The scientific views and their calculations are unchanged.
